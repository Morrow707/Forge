# Open questions for counsel

**These are questions, not text.** Each one used to sit inside a document as a
`[Placeholder -- ...]` bracket, which meant a parent reading /terms was being shown
Forge asking its own lawyer whether the clause above worked. A note to counsel inside
a document a user accepts is an admission printed on the thing it undermines, so the
questions live here and the clauses stand on their own.

Nothing here is resolved. They move out of this file when counsel answers them, and
the answer changes the clause rather than this list.

## 1. Terms of Service, s14 Indemnification

Confirm with counsel whether and how this section can apply where the person being asked to indemnify is a minor athlete or their parent/guardian; several states limit or void an indemnification obligation imposed on a minor.

## 2. Institutional Service Agreement

Counsel should confirm exactly what allocation of this responsibility is enforceable, and whether any additional Forge-side mechanism is needed to support it, rather than assuming a contract clause alone resolves it.

## 3. Institutional Service Agreement

This is the core liability-shifting mechanism of the whole Agreement and the single clause most in need of real counsel drafting; the consumer Terms of Service's own indemnification section carries a similar, still-unresolved flag for minor athletes, and that issue is not solved by moving the obligation onto the Institution instead.

## 4. Institutional Service Agreement

If the Institution's use case actually requires FERPA school-official status or a signed data-processing agreement, that is separate, unbuilt work, not something this paragraph accomplishes by itself.

## How these reach a reviewer

Alongside `docs/biometric-release-for-counsel.md`, which carries the video and
biometric consent plus the engineering facts behind each of its claims. The five
documents under review are the Terms of Service, the Privacy Policy, the EULA, the
Notice to Parent or Guardian and the Institutional Service Agreement; the four
documents people actually accept in the product -- the signup Terms of Use, the video
and biometric consent, the assumption of risk, and the AI Terms of Use -- are separate
and are not drafts.
