## THE HEADLINE: THE INSTRUMENT IS IN BETTER SHAPE THAN THE ARCHITECTURE AROUND IT

Part 1 of the check is the best-evidenced thing in the system. The daily, scored, coach-visible
FLAG is what the evidence refuses, and it refuses it on arithmetic rather than on physiology.

### TEMPER MEASURES SLEEP DEBT, NOT ACCUMULATED TRAINING LOAD, AND THAT IS A REFRAME NOT A KILL

Reaction time degrades reliably under **sleep loss**. It does not reliably degrade under
**accumulated training load**. The two studies that tested that properly both came out negative:

- **Costello et al. 2023**, *Eur J Sport Sci* 23(7):1334-1344, PMID 35771645. 23 trained male
  cyclists, 2 weeks intensified then a 2-week taper. Volume, load and strain up **108% / 116% /
  151%**; overreaching CONFIRMED -- peak power **-4.8%**, mean power **-9.4%**, total mood
  disturbance **+169%**. And: **"No change in any cognitive measure was observed across the study
  period."** Everything recovered after the taper. The authors advocate "caution in its
  utilisation as an indicator for the preliminary identification of overreaching."
- **Ten Haaf et al. 2018**, *IJSPP* 13(4):428-433, PMID 28872374. 30 subjects, 8-day cycling
  event, external load up ~900%, 14 acutely fatigued and 14 functionally overreached. The title
  is the finding: *"Changes in Choice Reaction Time During and After 8 Days Exhaustive Cycling
  Are Not Related to Changes in Physical Performance."* RT got FASTER -- practice dominated.

**CORRECTED #1 -- THE ONE REVIEW THAT LOOKS SUPPORTIVE OVERSTATES ITS OWN DATA, AND THIS WAS
VERIFIED IN THE ARTICLE'S OWN BODY TEXT RATHER THAN TAKEN ON AN AGENT'S WORD.**
Symons, Bruce & Main 2023, *Sports Med Open* 9:69, PMID 37552398, PMC10409951. Its abstract:
*"all seven studies finding that cognitive performance declined in response to athletes becoming
overreached or overtrained."* Its own body, read from the PMC full text:
- N-Back section: *"There were no significant differences between groups on the task performance,
  suggesting that although the athletes were fatigued, the physical exhaustion was insufficient
  to affect cognitive control."*
- Reaction Time section: *"although FOR athletes had slower reaction times at rest and after low
  intensity exercise, no significant differences were found when compared to control groups"*,
  and *"Significant differences for reaction time were found in three of the studies"* -- three
  of four, not seven of seven.
- *"It was not possible to determine whether the athletes in the Rietjens et al. were FOR/NFOR
  due to the absence of measures following a recovery period"*, and that study reports no numbers.
**Said fairly: an overstatement, not a fabrication.** Blain et al. 2019 did find increased
impulsivity (immediate over delayed rewards) and Hynynen et al. 2008 did find more Stroop errors.
The constructs that moved are not simple reaction time. Do not cite this review's abstract.

**CORRECTED #2 -- A PREMISE THIS SESSION HANDED THE REVIEWERS AS SETTLED WAS WRONG.**
"Mental fatigue degrades endurance performance" does not survive bias correction.
**Holgado, Sanabria, Perales & Vadillo 2020**, *J Cognition* 3(1):38, doi 10.5334/joc.126,
read at the publisher: performance **d_z 0.50 -> 0.08**, RPE 0.21 -> 0.10, both non-significant
after a three-parameter selection model, Egger's test significant for both. Verbatim:
*"current evidence does not provide conclusive support for the claim that mental fatigue has a
negative influence on exercise performance."* (A correction article to that paper exists.)
**What HAS held up is the strength/power null** (Van Cutsem et al. 2017, *Sports Med*
47(8):1569-1588, PMID 28044281) -- which is the half this repo already relies on.

**So Temper is pointed at the wrong target, not built wrong.** It reads sleep debt and acute
mental fatigue; an athlete who was up until 3am is the thing nothing else in Forge can see, which
is the design record's own argument for building it, and it survives. "Neuro-fatigue from
training" does not.

## PART 1 IS WHERE IT SHOULD BE, WITH ONE NAMED COST

Best-supported part of the system "and it is not close":
- A validated analogue (the PVT) and **no practice effect across 16 administrations including
  four a day** -- Basner, Hermosillo, Nasrini, McGuire et al. 2018, *Sleep* 41.
- Published day-to-day within-person noise in real units: **7.1 ms mean between-session
  difference at 100 trials**, learning effect <3 ms across three weekly sessions (Woods, Wyma,
  Yund & Herron 2015, *Front Hum Neurosci* 9:540; SRT ICC 0.84).
- **It works on children in a noisy real-world setting**: Wilson, Dollman, Lushington & Olds
  2010, *Behav Res Methods* 42:754-8, 75 boys mean 11.8y, Palm PDA in a CLASSROOM -- mean RT
  ICC 0.84, Bland-Altman showing no systematic difference between isolated and classroom
  settings. That is the single most relevant reliability result for a locker room.
- The band we actually live in, on people's **own phones**: Van Patten, Iverson, Muzeau &
  VanRavenhorst-Bell 2021, *Front Psychol* 12:734947, n=55, weekly x3 -- reliable change
  **+/-46.7 ms at 80% CI**, SEdiff 36.5 ms, and 60% of the sample naturally fell between
  **-23.2 and +53.2 ms** week to week.

**The cost, on the record: the median.** Basner & Dinges 2011 recommend mean and median RT
*"be avoided as primary measures of alertness"*, because the sleep-loss signal lives in the slow
tail a median is designed to ignore. This session measured the other side of that trade itself
(`tools/anvil-sim/recip.py`): one distracted trial moves the median 0.22 SD against mean 1/RT's
0.81 and the arithmetic mean's 1.70. **Both are true, so carry TWO numbers** -- the median as the
stable anchor plus a tail-sensitive metric with its own wider band, and a lapse threshold
calibrated PER PERSON rather than fixed at 355 ms.

**AND THE 25-VS-56-TRIAL QUESTION IS SETTLED AND THE OBVIOUS ANSWER IS WRONG.**
`tools/anvil-sim/budget.py`: parts 2-4 cost 117 s, which at 3.77 s a trial is 31 more trials, so
part 1 is 25 trials where the same check could run 56. Those 31 trials buy about **12%**, because
the median's own noise at n=25 (11.0 ms) is already smaller than the ~15.5 ms day-to-day spread
the +/-19 ms band implies. **The four-part split is not what is hurting the headline.** The design
record's 1 Nov conclusion -- "it needs more of the one trial that produces a measurement" -- is
refuted for this protocol, and the whole thing hinges on ONE unmeasured number: the athlete's own
between-day SD. No test-retest has ever been run.

## PART 2 IS THE WEAK ONE AND SHOULD CHANGE

- At **150** no-go trials the commission-error SDC is still 15-18 percentage points. We have
  **five** non-go trials. The score takes six values, so one lapse is a 20-point swing -- the
  measure is quantised coarser than its own published noise floor.
- The one phone study of this exact design found it the **least reliable test in its own battery**
  (ICC 0.60 over two weeks) with a 90% reliable-change band of **+/-121 ms on a 352 ms mean**,
  34% of the score.
- Inhibition is the least temporally stable behavioural measure in the literature, and the
  instability is not measurement error.
- **The press-and-hold condition has no published precedent as specified**, and its nearest
  analogues point the opposite way to the design's reasoning.
- **The fix is cheap**: at the current 31% non-go ratio, 15 no-go trials is ~48 trials, ~60 s.

## PART 4 IS THE ONE WITH NO EVIDENCE BASE AT ALL

- **The "CNS tap test" traces to a 2009 AAHPERD conference ABSTRACT, n=14 collegiate sprinters,
  two weeks, which its own authors called preliminary**, plus a training-blog observation study
  with an unreported sample size. No full publication followed.
- **Chaabouni et al. 2022**, *Int J Environ Res Public Health* 19(7):3792, PMID 35409476 --
  verified at the abstract. A foot-tapping test in 40 soccer players: **ICC > 0.90, SEM < 5%**,
  correlates **r = 0.84 with the finger tapping test**, discriminates national from regional
  players strongly (dz 1.96, **AUC 0.95**) -- and **"no correlation was seen between the TAP test
  and all the other physical tests measured"** (four jumps, sprints, agility, Wingate).
  **A precise measure of a stable TRAIT that predicts no performance** is the worst possible
  profile for daily within-person change, and it is the reliability paradox in one study.
- **TAP EVENNESS SHOULD GO.** Pawłowski et al. 2023, *J Hum Kinet* 86:51-61, PMID 37181260 --
  n=20, four blocks of 30 s jumping at 80% max, rhythm tested after each: *"The hypothesis about
  the significant effect of fatigue on the sense of rhythm was falsified."* It also fails to move
  under 200-400 mg caffeine. It is the least reliable thing the test produces, has almost no
  between-person signal, and has a direct null against a real fatiguing protocol.
- **A practice tap contaminates the score.** Heimhofer et al. 2026, *Front Hum Neurosci*
  20:1782120, PMID 42022243 -- 10 s of maximal tapping with ONE hand reduces the OTHER hand's
  initial speed by **1.7% +/- 3.2%** (30 s: 3.9%), supraspinal rather than peripheral. That is
  the size of the signal being hunted.
- If kept: **three 10-s trials** (MDC95 ~17% -> ~10%), a **dead band of +/-6%** on the three-trial
  mean (+/-10% on one), nothing inside the dead band produces a verdict, and the **lowest weight**
  in any composite.

## CAN WE ADD MORE GAMES? ONE, AND IT IS NOT A COGNITIVE ONE

The oculomotor/pupil end of the candidate list is built almost entirely on 20-40 hour total
sleep deprivation, which is a far bigger insult than a training week. **Only one candidate has a
pooled, meta-analysed, EXERCISE-induced effect.**

1. **Single-leg postural sway.** Penedo et al. 2026, *J Biomech* 198:113189, PMID 41650689 --
   41 studies, 702 young adults, SMD 0.41-3.10 (considerable heterogeneity, so read the span as
   protocols not as an expectation). And the only candidate with a published WITHIN-PERSON
   reliable change measured **remotely on people's own phones**: Caccese et al. 2022,
   *Front Digit Health* 4:999250, PMID 36405418 -- composite **ICC 0.88**, individual stances
   0.62-0.83, **reliable change 9 points of 100** at 80% CI.
   **Two hard constraints.** SINGLE-LEG only: Dewan et al. 2019, PMID 31930140, found the
   validated app "not sensitive enough to detect lower-magnitude postural sway changes in
   progressively challenging double leg stances", with near-maximal values and near-zero variance
   -- an outright ceiling. And read the **MEDIO-LATERAL** axis: da Silva et al. 2022,
   PMID 35222070, found ankle fatigue raised ML oscillations "with no significant effects in the
   AP axis", and found expert gymnasts NOT protected, which is good news for a test aimed at
   athletes.
2. **Voice, 15 seconds, and the reason is architectural.** Thoret, Andrillon, Gauriau, Léger &
   Pressnitzer 2024, *PLoS Comput Biol* 20(2):e1011849, PMID 38315733 -- under mild sleep
   restriction, leave-one-subject-out classification was **56% balanced accuracy (chance)** and
   **within-subject 86%** (SD 0.09), about half the participants above 90%. A channel that is
   useless as a population model and good as a personal one is the Anvil's exact shape. Caveats
   are real: 22 women aged 30-50, sleep restriction not training load, ~31 min of audio per
   person to train, and a noisy locker room attacks both discovered feature families.
3. **Already built and filed as a toy:** the Bellows' **Pursuit** ("keep your finger on the dot
   for 15 seconds, 900 samples of motor control") is continuous motor tracking sitting in the
   practice corner feeding nothing.

**RULED OUT, WITH REASONS, SO NOBODY RE-PROPOSES THEM:**
- **Pupillographic sleepiness test** -- best-evidenced sleepiness index on the list and
  incompatible: complete darkness, goggles, an infrared pupillometer, 5.5-11 minutes.
- **Pupillary light reflex** (the phone-feasible cousin) -- ruled out on EFFECT SIZE not
  feasibility: 28 hours awake moved constriction latency 298.6 -> 308.4 ms, **3.3%**, smaller
  than the phone's own measurement error.
- **King-Devick** -- the effect's SIGN flips between populations, and it IMPROVES on repetition
  ("both groups, on average, had significant improvement after 15 min (suggesting a learning
  effect)"); in wheelchair athletes scores improved 3.5% with exertion.
- **Critical flicker fusion** -- four studies, four directions (one found CFF INCREASED after
  exhaustive cycling and "did not correlate with central fatigue"). A 60-120 Hz panel has no
  headroom above a ~40 Hz threshold, and OLED PWM dimming adds its own flicker.
- **Touchscreen force steadiness** -- unbuildable: 3D Touch is gone from the hardware from the
  iPhone XR onward, replaced by a software long-press. No published work exists using a phone
  touchscreen for force steadiness.
- **Dual-task costs** -- a difference of two noisy measures carries BOTH variances, which is the
  opposite of what a daily within-person test needs, and roughly doubles the time cost.
- **Timing / rhythm** -- no evidence of acute fatigue effects in healthy people, and see the
  tap-evenness null above. The candidate that most deserves "good story, no data".
- **Saccadic peak velocity** -- the best MECHANISM on the list and still a research bet: the only
  direct test of a video-based device found FIT values after 30 and 36 h of wakefulness did not
  differ from baseline while EOG and subjective sleepiness both did; peak velocity is biased
  downward at low sampling rates and worst for small saccades, which a phone screen forces; and
  the one iPad validation reports latency, amplitude and rate -- **not** peak velocity.

## GRAIN COMES OUT AHEAD OF TEMPER, SO DESIGN-RECORD ITEM 57 IS WRONG

- **Grain's five attributes are almost exactly the ACSI-28 minus its Goal Setting subscale.** The
  constructs are real and sport-validated -- and Grain inherits that instrument's known
  psychometric weaknesses, which cluster on two of the five.
- **Self-report beats the objective channels for sensitivity to load.** Saw, Main & Gastin 2016,
  *BJSM* 50(5):281-291, PMID 26423706, 56 studies: subjective and objective measures "generally
  did not correlate", and subjective measures reflected acute and chronic load "with superior
  sensitivity and consistency". Thorpe's elite-soccer data: perceived fatigue r = -0.51 against
  daily high-intensity running, HRV -0.24, CMJ 0.23. Grainger et al. 2024, *Sports Med*
  54(4):855-874, PMID 38114782: in a rugby fatigue meta-analysis **soreness was the only
  significant marker (ES 0.91)** while CMJ height was not (24h ES -0.43, 95% CI **-3.99 to 3.21**).
- **The caveat to carry with it:** Duignan et al.'s review of single-item self-report found
  associations with load ranging from none to very large and **"predominantly trivial to moderate
  in the studies with the largest numbers of observations"** -- a classic small-study effect.
- **The gaming problem is documented, not speculative.** Neupert, Cotterill & Jobson 2019,
  *IJSPP*, PMID 29952658, nine national-team sprinters: training modifications perceived as
  disproportionate produced **"dishonest reporting practices"**. So: the moment Grain visibly
  reduces prescribed load, Grain stops measuring what it measured. **That is also the one real
  argument FOR the three-channel architecture** that the composite literature does not otherwise
  give us -- Temper and Drive are behavioural and kinematic, and much harder to game.

**So item 57's "move Grain out of the Anvil, beside Coaches Corner" is wrong on the evidence, and
item 58's demotion of Temper was if anything too gentle.** Grain is the better-supported channel
for the slow timescales; Temper is the one that works on a day with no training in it.

## DRIVE TAKES THE DAMAGE, AND IT IS THE CHANNEL WE JUST WIRED

- **Courel-Ibáñez et al. 2019**, *Ann Biomed Eng*, PMID 30980292 -- five technologies measured
  simultaneously, n=17: smallest detectable change for **smartphone-video bar velocity > 0.13
  m/s** against 0.02-0.06 for a linear transducer. On a bench mean velocity of ~0.5 m/s that is a
  **~26% detection floor**, against a fatigue effect of **4.0-12.5%** (Moura et al. 2024,
  PMID 38684188). **The noise floor is 2-6x the signal**, and that paper recommends smartphone
  video "should be avoided".
- **Banyard et al. 2017**, *JSCR* 31(7):1897-1904, PMID 27669192 -- actual 1RM is extremely
  stable across days (ICC 0.99, CV 2.1%) while **velocity at 1RM is not: ICC 0.42, CV 22.5%**,
  predicted 1RM varying -5.5% to +27.8% within individuals.
- **Velocity loss across a set fails as a between-day signal.** Jukic et al. 2023,
  *Sports Med Open* 9(1):80, PMID 37668949, **n=46**: agreement in work completed across two
  sessions "was not acceptable", moderated by sex, load, strength, training history and
  **emotional stability**.
- **Camera range of motion is the worst-measured variable in the review**: published phone-vs-LPT
  **ICC 0.236**, limits of agreement spanning ~31 cm. And ROM is partly BEHAVIOURAL -- the athlete
  chooses depth -- so a perfect instrument would not isolate fatigue.
- **Bar-path deviation: four PubMed queries, zero reliability or fatigue studies.** Any claim
  from it would be primary evidence we generated ourselves.
- **What survives for a daily claim: CMJ height from flight time, as the MEAN of >=3 trials.**
  Gençoğlu et al. 2023, PMID 37978338, 21 studies, supports phone flight-time measures; Bishop et
  al. 2022, PMID 36157951, n=27 vs force plate: jump height bias **0.001 m**, LoA +/-0.033 m, CV
  4.10%, ICC 0.96. Strategy variables are far worse -- raw time-to-take-off bias 0.075 s with LoA
  +/-0.157 s -- though **systematically** so, hence correctable, which is the one positive result
  for camera-derived timing.
- **AND IN OUR OWN DEMOGRAPHIC THE HOPED-FOR METRICS ARE THE ONES THAT FAIL.** Ruf et al. 2022,
  *Pediatr Exerc Sci* 34(3):125-134, PMID 35045392 -- **108 U12-U17 players**: only **jump height,
  concentric impulse and concentric velocity** had good reliability; most CMJ variables did not;
  reliability was WORSE at and before peak height velocity. The phase-duration and depth metrics
  predicted to "move first" are exactly the ones that break in 13-17 year olds.
- Honest Drive configuration: **FAST daily** = CMJ mean jump height only, >=3 trials, with a
  stated miss rate. **SLOW weekly/mesocycle** = mean concentric velocity at a fixed load,
  aggregated. Not seven of its eight signals.

## THE STATISTICS, WHERE THE CONCRETE FIXES ARE

**TWO INDEPENDENT SIMULATIONS AGREE THE ROLLING BASELINE DOUBLES THE FALSE-ALARM RATE.** This
session's (`tools/anvil-sim/calibrate.py`) measured single-day flags 6.4 -> 13.1 going from a
known sigma to a rolling-20 median/MAD, a **2.05x** inflation; a reviewer's independent
simulation got **2.06x** (p(z>=2.0) 0.0233 -> 0.0470). Converging from different code is the
reason to believe it.

- **The bias half is one constant.** `MAD/0.6745` is the ASYMPTOTIC relation; on 20 points the
  MAD runs **4.0% low**, so every z is ~4% high and the 2.0 threshold is really **1.92**.
  Measured corrections: **1.0969 at n=10, 1.0616 at 14, 1.0420 at 20, 1.0267 at 30, 1.0135 at 60**.
  Fix this regardless of what happens to the architecture.
- **The variance half no constant fixes.** MAD's asymptotic efficiency at the normal is ~37%, so
  MAD on 20 points behaves like an SD on ~7, and a z over a wobbling scale has fat tails.
  Nominal-rate equivalents at W=20: **z >= 2.48** (for p=0.0228) and **z >= 1.43** (for p=0.1056).
- **As specified, the rules fire 16.9 times a season on an athlete who never changes**, 100% of
  athletes flagged at least once, ~508 false flags on a 30-athlete roster -- one every 8.3
  athlete-checks (`falsealarm.py`). Raising the threshold is a bad trade: z>=3.5 is ten times
  quieter and catches a quarter as much.
- **THE ROLLING BASELINE HIDES REAL DECLINE ALMOST PERFECTLY.** A reviewer's drift simulation: a
  **3-SD** season-long deterioration produces ~3 extra flag-days above the noise floor on a
  rolling-20 baseline (1.5x signal-to-noise) against **6.7x on a fixed anchor**. **The SLOW fixed
  anchor is doing the work FAST is assumed to be doing.** Standard SPC doctrine, and the argument
  for CUSUM.
- **CORROBORATION BEATS A HIGHER THRESHOLD, AND IT IS RULE #4 POINTED AT THE ANVIL.** Both routes
  measured on ONE scenario (`matched_route.py`; a real 1.0-SD drop held 7 checks, against a
  140-check season in which nothing happens):

      one channel, z>=2.0 (as specified)   15.6 false/season   68.0% caught   4.4 per false
      one channel, z>=3.0                   3.9               28.8%          7.4
      2 of 3 channels, z>=2.0               4.8               47.0%          9.8
      2 of 3, real availability .5/.4       1.8               22.2%         12.4

  At a MATCHED alarm rate corroboration catches **1.6x** as much, and caught-per-false-flag is
  better at every point. **An unmatched comparison would have claimed 2.8x**; recorded because
  that is the error this repo flags in other people's work.
  **AND THE LAST ROW IS THE CATCH:** corroboration goes nearly mute when channels are absent,
  which on this product they constantly are. So the honest rule is a corroborated claim on the
  days two channels have data and **"one witness, not a finding"** otherwise -- which is what
  `AnvilReading` / `AnvilAbsence` in `shared/anvil-inputs.ts` already encodes.
- **Magnitude-based inference is formally refuted** (Welsh & Knight PMID 25051387; Sainani
  PMID 29683920). Do not build the decision rule on its language.
- **The disjoint-window fix is partial.** It removes Lolli's mathematical coupling, but
  Impellizzeri et al., PMID 32502973, state ACWR remains "an inaccurate metric (failing to
  normalize the numerator by the denominator **even when uncoupled**)" that "adds noise and
  creates statistical artifacts". Carry load and change-in-load as separate covariates, not a
  ratio. And say which question SLOW asks: "declining GIVEN their training" (adjust) or
  "declining" (do not) -- load is exposure, dose and confounder depending on the question.
- **CAREER needs a maturity covariate before it means anything in 13-16s.** Maturity spread at a
  fixed chronological age is +/-2 years; peak rate of improvement is **13.2y for vertical jump and
  13.8y for endurance**, right inside our band. `NORM_MIN_COHORT` of 30 is fine as a suppression
  floor and far below what a stable centile needs (published youth norms use ~2.8M performances).
- **The recommended replacement for the whole FAST rule is a per-athlete CUSUM or EWMA calibrated
  to a stated average run length.** It addresses the threshold, the autocorrelation and the
  gradual-drift blindness at once.

## WHAT THE EVIDENCE SAYS ABOUT SHIPPING THIS AT ALL

- **THE OUTCOME TEST HAS BEEN RUN IN OUR POPULATION AND IT WAS NULL.** Dalen-Lorentsen et al.
  2021, *BJSM*, PMID 33036995 -- cluster-randomised, **34 elite youth football teams, 482 players,
  both sexes, a full 10-month season**, intervention coaches planning all training with ACWR load
  management through a **commercial athlete-management system**. Health-problem prevalence
  difference **1.8 percentage points (95% CI -4.1 to 7.7), RR 1.01 (0.91-1.12)**.
- **THE HARM CHANNEL IS LARGER THAN THE BENEFIT CHANNEL, AND IT CLOSES A LOOP THROUGH OUR OWN
  GAMES.** Monitoring-guided training's own performance benefit is **g ~ 0.08-0.26,
  non-significant** (Düking PMID 34489178, 8 studies/198 participants; Manresa-Rocamora
  PMID 34639599 -- "if HRV-guided training is superior... it is only by a small margin"). The
  nocebo effect on motor performance is **mean ES 0.60** across 23 studies (Horváth, Köteles &
  Szabo 2021, PMID 34145580), "may be more robust than the placebo effect". And
  **Draganich & Erdal 2014, "Placebo sleep affects cognitive functioning"**, PMID 24417326, n=164:
  people randomised to be TOLD they had below-average REM sleep performed worse on PASAT and
  COWAT, while **self-reported actual sleep quality did not predict performance**. So a displayed
  low score degrades tomorrow's Temper, which a rolling-20 then reads as corroboration.
- **Individual prediction is near chance.** Ruddy et al. 2018, PMID 29266094: within-year median
  AUC 0.58 and 0.57, **between-year (true out-of-sample) 0.52**. Bullock et al. 2022,
  PMID 35689749: 30 studies, **204 injury-prediction models, zero externally validated**, 98%
  high or unclear risk of bias, **"No models could be recommended for use in practice."**
  Treat any published AUC above ~0.7 in this field as within-sample until proven otherwise.
- **"Neural fatigue" has no field referent.** Central fatigue is operationally defined by twitch
  interpolation and TMS (Gandevia 2001 PMID 11581501; Taylor et al. 2016 PMID 27003703, who note
  the changes "have mostly been investigated during single-limb isometric contractions"). None of
  it is reachable from a phone.
- **No marker or panel diagnoses overtraining.** ECSS/ACSM consensus, Meeusen et al. 2013,
  PMID 23247672: distinguishing NFOR from OTS "will depend on the clinical outcome and **exclusion
  diagnosis**", and of the markers in use "**none of them meet all the criteria to make their use
  generally accepted**".
- **Base rates make most flags false.** 29% of 376 athletes mean age 15.1 reported NFOR/OT at
  least once in their LIFETIME (Matos, Winsley & Williams 2011, PMID 21131861) -- and in that
  study **training load was not a significant predictor**. At a plausible per-season incidence and
  the measured AUCs, PPV lands around 7-17%.
- **Youth cognitive testing: the guidance is against a daily score.** ImPACT -- the leading
  commercial battery, administered a few times a year by clinicians -- **misclassifies 5-26% of
  healthy retests as a "reliable change"** (Alsalaheen et al. 2016, PMID 26291631), with ICC <0.80
  for every composite but processing speed. The 6th International Consensus Statement on
  Concussion in Sport (Patricios et al. 2023, *BJSM* 57(11):695-711, PMID 37316210) is reported to
  say baseline testing "is of limited use in younger athletes because of neurocognitive
  development" and that computerised neurocognitive tests "are not to be used in isolation" --
  **the article is confirmed real but paywalled, so those sentences are a reviewer's reading of
  the full text and were NOT independently verified here.**
- **Athletes manipulate self-report and the psychological cost lands on our users.** Saw et al.
  2017, PMID 29910414, 335 athletes over 16 weeks: confidence and extrinsic motivation rose while
  **satisfaction and intrinsic motivation fell at week 4**, and "novice athletes and those who
  were **instructed** to use an ASRM (rather than using one autonomously) were less responsive".
  A 15-year-old told by school staff to complete a daily score is the instructed novice.
- **What the youth literature DOES support, and we already collect the input.** Monasterio et al.
  2024, PMID 38188110, 84 academy players over 20 seasons: growth rate **>7.2 cm/yr** predicted
  growth-related injury at every maturity stage, and post-PHV **slow** growers carried **2.4x the
  incidence and 2.6x the burden** of joint/ligament injury. A monthly standing height has a better
  youth evidence base than any of the Anvil's three channels.
- **Prior art is unvalidated.** Targeted searches found **no peer-reviewed validation of any
  commercial composite readiness score** (WHOOP recovery, Garmin Body Battery, the lifters' CNS
  tap test). The underlying SENSORS have a real literature; the proprietary score on top does not.
  And the closest published game-based analogue reports its own limits honestly: Shepherd et al.
  2022 on Highmark's Fast Ball give session-to-session Bland-Altman limits of **-195.81 to
  +113.59 ms**, with not one of seven subtests reaching "good" reliability.

## PRACTICE IS THE FIRST-ORDER PROBLEM, AND IT LANDS INSIDE BOTH BASELINE WINDOWS

The eleventh review changes the ordering of the risks. **Practice is roughly twenty times larger
and twenty times faster than adolescent maturation over this horizon.** On a daily smartphone
processing-speed task, healthy controls improved **16.6% (delta 7.75 points, ~1.1 SD) in a median
of 10-11 repetitions, about 21 days**, before plateauing (de Jong et al. 2026, *J Neurol*,
PMID 42096072). Over the same SIX MONTHS a 14-year-old's developmental gain on comparable
measures is about **d 0.03-0.07** (derived from Cromer et al. 2015, *Front Psychol* 6:704,
PMID 26074853). Maturation is real and second-order; practice is first-order.

**AND IT IS CONCENTRATED IN THE FIRST TWO TO FIVE WEEKS -- EXACTLY THE WINDOW IN WHICH BOTH OF
OUR BASELINES GET ESTABLISHED, AND THEY FAIL IN OPPOSITE DIRECTIONS.** The rolling-20 is built
during the steepest improvement, so it chases the gain; the fixed early-season anchor is SET at an
artificially poor level, so every athlete looks like they improve all season. Neither is a bug in
the statistics -- it is the input.

**THIS IS ALSO THE CLEANEST VINDICATION OF PART 1 IN THE WHOLE PASS, BECAUSE THE EFFECT IS
TASK-SPECIFIC.**

- **Simple RT is genuinely practice-resistant.** Woods, Wyma, Yund & Herron 2015, PMID 26617505:
  "No significant learning effects were observed across test sessions", SRT differing **< 3 ms**
  across three weekly sessions. Del Rossi et al. 2014, *J Athl Train* 49(3):356-359,
  PMID 24673236, ten sessions over five weeks: 264.9 -> 252.0 ms, with the whole move in session
  1 -> 2 (7.2 ms, p = .057) and **no differences among the last nine**. Their recommendation is
  the cheap fix: **allow one practice session before recording a baseline.**
- **Go/no-go is the worst of the four, on a second axis independent of its trial count.** The
  closest measured analogue fell **31-33 ms with omega_p2 = 0.24 over three weekly sessions and
  HAD NOT PLATEAUED**, with the gains **largest on the hardest trials -- the ones carrying the
  inhibition signal**. So practice eats the signal preferentially. Combined with ~4-6 no-go trials
  quantizing commission-error rate into ~20-point steps: **no baseline policy can rescue a measure
  that coarse.** Either >=60 trials with >=20 no-go, or report only its GO-trial RT and drop the
  inhibition claim entirely.
- **Tapping: the touchscreen decides which world we are in, and we are in the bad one.**
  Mechanical max-rate tapping was flat across **ten weekly sessions** (Gill et al. 1986,
  r ~ .86-.94). But **touchscreen** tapping gained **+9.9 taps (~5-12%)** between two visits a
  week apart and **tightened inter-tap SD by 18%** (Makai-Bölöni et al. 2021), and a phone
  finger-movement test showed an **86.2% practice effect needing 94 repetitions** to reach 90% of
  its ceiling (Woelfle et al. 2021). **That 18% is a SECOND, INDEPENDENT reason to delete tap
  evenness**: practice tightens it in exactly the direction a "fresher" reading would move, so the
  metric's own learning curve is indistinguishable from the thing it claims to measure.
- **Spatial span is low practice risk ONLY because we re-randomize.** +2.4%, non-significant,
  across three weekly sessions, attributed directly to re-randomizing layout and sequences every
  trial -- so that property is load-bearing and must not be "simplified" away. But within-person
  reliability of CHANGE on smartphone span tasks is **0.17-0.48**, and a 3->7 integer span is
  coarse and will ceiling for older athletes.

**Which means the ordering of the four parts, on evidence, is: part 1, then part 3 (if randomized,
as a coarse trend), then part 4 (three trials, dead band, lowest weight, no evenness), then part 2
(fix the trial count or drop the inhibition claim).** The 12% the trial-budget simulation says
part 1 would gain from the other three parts' time looks different in this light: the other three
parts are not merely a poor use of the budget, two of them are actively contaminated over exactly
the horizon the Anvil runs on.

### ADDITIONS TO THE DECISION LIST

13. **Burn in the baseline: discard the first week of checks, or run one practice session before
    the first recorded one.** Cheapest fix in the pass, and the one the simple-RT literature
    recommends in its own words.
14. **Keep the sequence task's per-trial re-randomization.** It is the only reason part 3's
    practice curve is flat, and it reads like an implementation detail.
15. **Delete tap evenness on two independent grounds** -- it does not move under a real fatiguing
    protocol, and touchscreen practice tightens it 18% in the direction that mimics freshness.

## OPEN AND SCOTT'S: THE COLD-JUMP RESERVATION (2026-11-06)

A daily countermovement jump was proposed as the headline, on the grounds that CMJ height is the
one Drive signal the evidence clears for a daily within-person claim, that it is one of only three
CMJ variables with good reliability in 108 U12-U17 players, and that **"Countermovement Jump" is
already a video-eligible exercise** -- so it already has its own registry record, a posture, a ROM
bucket, a first move and film guidance, and `measureCountermovement` already reads the dip depth
and the eccentric and concentric durations and velocities.

**Scott: "the verbiage is sound but the cold factor is throwing me, note it, we will discuss
further, later."** So this is OPEN and it is his, not settled either way. Recorded so the next
session does not re-propose it as though it were new, and does not treat the counter-argument
below as having won.

**His objection:** an athlete taking a daily CMJ would not be warm, so the measurement is of a
sub-par performance.

**The counter-argument that was offered, stated plainly rather than as a conclusion:** a z-score
against the athlete's own rolling baseline cancels a bias that is present every day, and the Drive
review's own framing is that a biased-but-precise instrument is fine for change detection while an
unbiased-but-noisy one is not. **That argument addresses the LEVEL and does not address what may
be the real problem**, which is that warm-up state is not constant day to day -- in from the car,
after ten minutes of mobility, mid-practice -- and that variance is correlated with the testing
situation, so it biases rather than blurs, the same structural shape as caffeine and time of day.
A fixed set of submaximal practice jumps before the recorded trials was proposed as the remedy
(it would also fix trial-1-is-lowest, which the mean-of-three rule otherwise inherits).

**WHAT IS NOT IN DISPUTE AND SHOULD NOT BE RE-DERIVED:**
- **The timing requirement.** Flight-time height squares the error (`h = g*t^2/8`, so
  `dh/h = 2*dt/t`). At the live path's ~30Hz, one frame on a 35cm jump is **12.5%** and two frames
  **25.0%**, against a fatigue effect of 7.5-10.4% -- the quantisation alone swamps it. At 120fps
  it is **3.1% / 6.2%**, under the instrument's own published CV of 4.10%. The design record
  already names the fix: read the times off the **file path**, which decodes the full 120fps clip
  after the set, rather than the live path. A jump is not a live-feedback drill. Already built.
- The mean of three trials multiplies the timing noise by **0.58**, which is the same thing
  Claudino's mean-not-best rule asks for.
- **The costs, which are real whatever is decided:** three maximal jumps plus a practice set is
  30-60 seconds of daily effort and friction is what kills these products (the comparable figure
  is 5% finishing fifteen days in a SUPERVISED classroom); it needs floor space and somewhere to
  prop a phone; and a daily maximal jump is itself a small training stimulus.
- **And the naming consequence**, which is why the two questions are one question: if the JUMP is
  the headline rather than the cognitive games, "neuromuscular" is an earned word on the published
  vocabulary (the verified paper is titled "Alternative countermovement-jump analysis to quantify
  acute **neuromuscular fatigue**"). The name Scott wanted to keep becomes defensible precisely by
  moving the headline to the channel the word belongs to. If the jump is NOT the headline, that
  argument is not available and the naming question returns to where the decision list left it.

**The number that would settle the cold question is unmeasured, and it is the same number
everything else here waits on:** the between-day SD of a phone CMJ on this pipeline, taken under
the real conditions -- unwarmed, varying warm-up, a locker-room floor. No test-retest of any Anvil
task has ever been run. One filming week answers it, and until it is run both positions are
arguments rather than findings.
